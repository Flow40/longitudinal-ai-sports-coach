# Paper Outline

## Working Title

**From One-Off Prompts to a Persistent AI Sports Coach: Lessons from a Long-Term Human–LLM Case Study**

## 1. Introduction

- limitations of episodic AI assistance
- longitudinal nature of coaching
- motivation for persistent memory

## 2. Problem Statement

- personalization across time
- changing goals, injuries and environments
- memory consistency and safety

## 3. System Design

- memory categories
- update process
- planning loop
- user feedback loop

## 4. Methodology

- single-user longitudinal case study
- types of recorded information
- qualitative comparison framework
- anonymization approach

## 5. Case Studies

- shoulder injury and return to training
- lumbar constraints
- hotel-gym adaptation
- load progression based on completed work

## 6. Observations

- continuity
- personalization
- relevance
- exercise substitution
- progression quality

## 7. Safety and Privacy

- medical boundaries
- escalation
- sensitive data
- correction and deletion

## 8. Limitations

- single participant
- no control group
- reporting bias
- hallucinations
- memory drift

## 9. Future Work

- structured evaluation
- wearable integration
- coach comparison
- multi-agent approaches
- privacy-preserving memory

## 10. Conclusion

The central hypothesis is that persistent, structured memory may be more important than increasingly complex prompting for long-term personalized AI assistance.
