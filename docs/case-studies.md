# Case Studies

These examples are anonymized and simplified.

## Case Study 1 — Return After Shoulder Dislocation

### Context

The athlete returned to training after a right shoulder dislocation and four weeks of immobilization.

### Coaching Adaptation

The system:

- removed dips and high-risk overhead work
- favored incline dumbbell pressing and controlled pulling
- reintroduced pressing progressively
- tracked pain after each session
- avoided side-plank variations that irritated the shoulder

### Long-Term Value

A memory-aware system could avoid repeatedly suggesting movements already identified as unsuitable.

## Case Study 2 — Lumbar Constraints

### Context

The athlete had chronic lumbar sensitivity and imaging-confirmed degenerative changes.

### Coaching Adaptation

The system:

- reduced heavy hinge exposure
- limited aggressive deadlift progression
- used single-leg work and controlled hip-dominant movements
- prioritized trunk stability
- replaced sit-ups when they increased lumbar discomfort

### Long-Term Value

The objective of the session was preserved while exercise selection changed.

## Case Study 3 — Hotel Gym Session

### Context

The athlete was travelling and had access only to dumbbells and a limited cable machine.

### Coaching Adaptation

The session was rebuilt around:

- incline dumbbell press
- supported rowing
- light unilateral work
- time-efficient supersets
- shoulder-safe movement ranges

### Long-Term Value

The recommendation considered both available equipment and the athlete's recent injury history.

## Case Study 4 — Progression From Completed Work

### Context

The athlete regularly reported prescribed versus completed loads, repetitions, pain and RPE.

### Coaching Adaptation

The system used previous results to:

- maintain a load after high RPE
- increase load after stable technique and low pain
- reduce volume during travel fatigue
- preserve intensity when total training time was limited

### Long-Term Value

Progression was based on actual performance rather than on generic percentage increases.
